## What usually gets passed on

In most growing SaaS companies, a deal closes and the handover is a Slack message or a few lines in the CRM. The salesperson knows what was promised, who the real decision-maker is and which integration the customer is counting on. The onboarding team gets a company name and a start date.

## What it costs

The onboarding manager spends the first week asking questions the salesperson already knows the answers to. The customer repeats themselves to a second person. Go-live slips because a promised integration turns out to be a piece of custom work that nobody scoped.

> The short version: many late go-lives start with information that was missing at the handover.

## How to tell it is happening

- Onboarding asks the same first five questions on every new customer.
- Customers say they already told your sales team.
- Go-live dates are set before anyone has checked what was promised.
- Nobody can say, in one place, what a given customer bought.

## What to do this week

You do not need new software to start. Four small steps are enough to see where the gaps are.

- Write down the ten facts onboarding always has to chase.
- Make them required fields on the deal, so it cannot move to signed without them.
- Hold a 20-minute handover call between sales and onboarding for deals above a size you choose.
- Track days from signed to live for the next ten customers, and note why the slow ones were slow.

## When it needs more than a checklist

If the same gaps keep appearing after a month, the cause is usually in how the CRM, the contract and the onboarding tool connect with each other. Fixing that takes some design, and the fix often involves more than one team.
